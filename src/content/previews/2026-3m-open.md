## The five-minute version

TPC Twin Cities looks like a survival test and plays like a shootout. Water
touches most of the property, which is what the eye sees first and what the
broadcast will show all weekend — but the water almost never sits between a
player and a good score. The real examination is quieter and happens with a
wedge in hand.

If you take one thing into your own read this week, take this: **the winner will
be decided from inside 150 yards.** Not off the tee, not on the greens
particularly — on the short-iron approaches that this course serves up again and
again. Sort the field by who is sharpest from there and you have most of your
answer.

## What the course demands

Read the demands panel below as a golfer, not a statistician. A high driving-
distance bar would mean length separates the field; here it doesn't, because the
fairways are wide enough that the bombers can't press their advantage. The bar
that matters is the wedge — the short-approach buckets are the tallest on the
card, and they are tall for a reason.

Rough is grown up enough to matter on the holes that flirt with water, but this
is not a course that punishes a slightly crooked tee shot. It punishes a lazy
wedge. The greens are large and only moderately contoured, so putts fall — which
is exactly why the birdie ceiling is so high and why nobody backs their way into
this title. You have to keep making them.

## Who fits, and who the profile quietly hurts

The fit ratings below are a read on how a player's skills line up with what this
specific course pays for — not a prediction, and not a pick. A five means the
skills and the venue are in lockstep. A three on a talented player is usually the
interesting case: it means the market is charging for ability the course is about
to neutralise.

That's the story with the bombers. A long, aggressive player earns a real edge
most weeks off the tee — and gives most of it back here, where the corridors are
generous and the second shot decides everything. The prices don't always reflect
that, which is where a reader with course intuition can see something the board
is slow to.

## Weather: the one variable that changes the math

Wind is a minor character at TPC Twin Cities most years — this is inland,
tree-lined parkland, not a links. But "minor" is not "none," and the one place it
bites is exactly where the danger already lives: a gust off the water while a
player is standing over a wedge to a tucked pin turns a routine approach into a
real decision. Watch the afternoon wind on the days it climbs. The wind rose
below plots hourly speed and direction across all four days so you can see when,
and from where, it's likely to press.

## Where the market looks fair versus stretched

See the market notes in the sidebar for the three prices worth having an opinion
about. The short version: the favourites are honest, one mid-board number
(Brandt) looks a tier too long, and one tempting price (Pruitt) is a fair number
for the wrong skill set. None of that is a recommendation to bet — it's where the
gap between the price and the profile is wide enough to be worth your own second
look.
